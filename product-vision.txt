TEMPLAR SOC — Product Vision

The SOC is not another SIEM.

It is not another CTF platform.

It is not another investigation engine.

Splunk already provides all of that.

Our job is to build a custom Templar SOC application that sits on top of Splunk and transforms the experience into something players will remember.

The application should feel like entering the classified Security Operations Center of the Templar Order. Players should completely forget they are using Splunk.

We will first build the complete UI/UX of this custom application. Once the experience is approved, we will integrate it with the existing Splunk functionality. The UI must be designed so it can later reuse native Splunk components instead of replacing them.

The Player Experience

Players do not arrive here directly.

They first complete the Active Directory challenge and recover valid Templar SOC credentials.

Using those credentials, they enter the captured Templar Security Operations Center.

From this moment, they stop being attackers.

They become investigators using the enemy's own SOC.

The purpose of the SOC is only to investigate historical telemetry generated by the Rogue Assassin attack.

Players search logs, follow evidence, read alerts, consume intelligence, and discover answers.

All answers are submitted back to the CTF portal.

The SOC never handles scoring, flags, progression, or case management.

It is simply the investigation workstation.

Player Views

The player application should contain only three primary modules.

1. Investigation Console

This is the heart of the application.

Almost all player time is spent here.

It should feel like a professional analyst workstation where investigators execute SPL searches, inspect results, pivot through evidence, and reconstruct attacker activity.

This is not a dashboard.

This is a workspace.

Everything else exists to support this screen.

2. Alerts

This represents the operational alert feed of the Templar SOC.

These alerts are not automatically generated.

During the live event, organizers manually publish alerts to all player sessions.

The purpose is to create pressure and make the SOC feel alive.

Examples include emergency broadcasts, suspicious activity notifications, infrastructure warnings, and operational messages.

Players read them exactly as analysts would inside a real SOC.

3. Threat Intelligence

This represents the classified intelligence division of the Templar Order.

Like Alerts, nothing is automatically generated.

Organizers manually publish intelligence throughout the competition.

Threat Intelligence should feel like reading confidential analyst reports rather than viewing dashboards.

It may include:

IOC updates
Campaign intelligence
New attacker techniques
Tactical guidance
Infrastructure observations
Emergency intelligence bulletins

This page should look like a classified intelligence briefing.

Mission Control (Admin Only)

Players must never access this interface.

Mission Control is available only to organizers.

This is where organizers operate the live SOC during the event.

Mission Control allows organizers to:

Publish operational alerts
Publish threat intelligence
Manage live broadcasts
Control what information players receive during the investigation

Nothing is automated.

Everything is intentionally controlled by the organizers to shape the story and increase immersion.

Players should believe they are watching a real Templar SOC responding to an ongoing cyber operation, even though every alert and intelligence update is being manually orchestrated behind the scenes.

Overall Goal

The SOC should never feel like a generic cybersecurity dashboard.

It should feel like a real military cyber command center belonging to the Templar Order.

Players should remember the experience as "the SOC where we investigated the Rogue Assassin intrusion", not "the Splunk app we used during the CTF."
